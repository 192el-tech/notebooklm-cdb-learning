## 2. What Is a CDB?

**CDB (Certificado de Depósito Bancário)** is a fixed-income security issued by financial institutions to raise funds for their activities.

When investing in a CDB, the investor effectively lends money to the issuing institution. In return, the investor receives the invested amount plus the agreed financial return according to the conditions of the product.

CDBs can differ considerably in terms of profitability, liquidity, maturity, and remuneration model. Therefore, understanding these characteristics is important before choosing a specific product.

### Key Characteristics

| Characteristic | Description |
|---|---|
| **Issuer** | Financial institution issuing the CDB |
| **Investment Type** | Fixed income |
| **Profitability** | Can be pre-fixed, post-fixed, or hybrid |
| **Liquidity** | Depends on the specific CDB |
| **Maturity** | Defined according to the product |
| **Taxation** | Subject to applicable Income Tax rules |
| **FGC Protection** | Available for eligible products within applicable limits |

## 3. How to Invest in a CDB

The process of investing in a CDB is generally straightforward:

### Step-by-Step

1. **Open an account**  
   Choose a bank or brokerage firm and complete the registration process.

2. **Transfer funds**  
   Deposit the amount you intend to invest into the investment account.

3. **Access the Fixed Income section**  
   Navigate to the investment platform and look for available fixed-income products.

4. **Compare CDBs**  
   Evaluate profitability, liquidity, maturity, minimum investment, and the issuing institution.

5. **Select the product**  
   Choose a CDB that matches your objectives, time horizon, and risk considerations.

6. **Make the investment**  
   Enter the desired amount and confirm the transaction.

7. **Monitor or redeem**  
   Depending on the product, the investment can either remain invested until maturity or be redeemed according to its liquidity conditions.

## 4. Main Advantages of CDBs

CDBs can offer several advantages, particularly for investors looking for accessible fixed-income products.

| Advantage | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Profitability** | CDBs can offer returns that are competitive with or higher than traditional savings accounts, depending on the product and market conditions. |
| **Safety** | Eligible CDBs are covered by the FGC within the applicable protection limits and conditions. |
| **Accessibility** | Some products have relatively low minimum investment requirements. |
| **Liquidity** | Some CDBs offer daily liquidity, while others require the investor to wait until maturity. |
| **Flexibility** | Investors can choose between different remuneration models, maturities, and liquidity conditions. |
| **Practicality** | CDBs can generally be purchased and monitored through digital banking or investment platforms. |

## 5. Types of CDB

CDBs can use different remuneration models.

### 5.1 Pre-Fixed CDB

The interest rate is defined when the investment is made.

**Main characteristic:** greater predictability of the contracted return when the investment is held according to its conditions.

### 5.2 Post-Fixed CDB

The return is linked to a reference indicator, commonly the CDI.

**Main characteristic:** the return follows the behavior of the reference indicator during the investment period.

### 5.3 Hybrid CDB

Combines a fixed interest rate with an inflation-related index, such as the IPCA.

**Main characteristic:** combines a fixed return component with an inflation-related component.

### Comparison

| Type | How It Works | Main Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Fixed** | Uses a previously defined interest rate | Greater predictability |
| **Post-Fixed** | Follows a reference indicator such as CDI | Follows market conditions |
| **Hybrid** | Combines a fixed rate with an inflation-related index | Combines fixed return and inflation reference |

## 6. Important Factors Before Investing

Choosing a CDB should involve more than simply looking at the highest advertised return.

### 💰 Profitability

Compare the expected return offered by different products and consider how it relates to the investment period and other available alternatives.

### 🔓 Liquidity

Check when the money can be redeemed.

CDBs with daily liquidity may be more suitable when frequent access to the funds is important, while products without immediate liquidity may require the investor to wait until maturity.

### 📅 Maturity

The maturity date indicates when the investment reaches the end of its contractual term.

Always verify whether the investment period matches your financial objective.

### 🛡️ Safety

Consider the financial institution issuing the CDB and understand the applicable FGC protection rules and limits.

FGC protection does not eliminate the need to evaluate the institution and the characteristics of the investment.

### 🧾 Taxation

CDB returns are subject to applicable taxation rules. Income Tax is generally calculated according to the duration of the investment, following a regressive structure.

Investors should also consider possible IOF taxation when applicable.

### 🎯 Investment Objective

The most attractive CDB is not necessarily the one with the highest nominal return.

The investment should be evaluated according to:

- Financial objective
- Investment horizon
- Liquidity needs
- Expected return
- Risk considerations
- Taxation
- Issuing institution

## 7. Traditional vs. Digital Banks

The experiments also explored whether the type of banking institution can influence the investment experience.

The financial product itself does not fundamentally change simply because it is offered by a traditional or digital institution. However, differences may exist in terms of accessibility, costs, product availability, customer support, and investment experience.

| Dimension | Traditional Banks | Digital Banks |
|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility** | May have higher entry requirements for some products | Often offer lower minimum investment requirements |
| **Yield** | May offer more conservative rates on some products | May offer competitive rates to attract investors |
| **Fees** | May include account or service-related fees depending on the institution | Often emphasize low-fee or fee-free digital services |
| **Advisory** | Greater availability of human advisory in some institutions | More emphasis on self-directed digital investing |
| **User Experience** | Combines physical branches with digital platforms | Primarily app- and platform-based |
| **Product Ecosystem** | Broad range of banking and investment services | Increasingly broad digital investment ecosystems |
| **Customer Support** | Can include physical and specialized support | Primarily digital support channels |

### Key Takeaway

The choice between a traditional and a digital bank should not be based solely on profitability.

Investors should also consider:

- Fees and costs
- Liquidity
- Product availability
- Institutional risk
- Customer support
- Investment accessibility
- Need for professional advisory

## 8. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **CDB** | Certificado de Depósito Bancário, a fixed-income security issued by financial institutions. |
| **CDI** | A reference indicator widely used in Brazilian fixed-income investments. |
| **FGC** | Fundo Garantidor de Créditos, a mechanism that provides protection for eligible deposits and investments within applicable limits and conditions. |
| **Fixed Income** | Investment category in which the remuneration follows predefined or reference-based conditions. |
| **Liquidity** | The conditions and ease with which an investment can be converted into available funds. |
| **Maturity** | The date on which an investment reaches the end of its contractual term. |
| **Pre-Fixed** | Investment whose interest rate is defined when the investment is made. |
| **Post-Fixed** | Investment whose return follows a reference indicator. |
| **Hybrid** | Investment combining a fixed interest rate with an inflation-related index. |
| **Yield / Profitability** | The financial return generated by an investment. |
| **Credit Risk** | The possibility that an issuer may fail to meet its financial obligations. |
| **IR** | Income Tax applicable to investment earnings according to the applicable tax rules. |
| **IOF** | A tax that may apply to certain financial operations, including specific early redemptions. |
| **Maturity Date** | The date established for the end of the investment term. |
